Put a Bug on It

Role Senior QA Analyst
Timeline March 2024 – May 2024
Tools Jira, Confluence, Perforce
Quality Assurance Tools

Overview

There are many issues with implementing an in-game bug reporter. At Intrepid Studios, I took time to figure out how to improve a Bug Reporter for a Live Service Title.

The Problem

In-game bug reporters for live service titles carry competing constraints: they must be low-friction enough that players actually use them, structured enough that the reports are actionable, and robust enough to survive the chaotic conditions of an active game session. The existing reporter at Intrepid Studios was none of these things. Report quality was low, triage was slow, and the tool itself was a source of bugs in its own right.

Process

I reviewed a sample of historical reports to categorise failure modes — missing reproduction steps, wrong category assignment, duplicate submissions, reports that were actually feedback rather than bugs. Each failure type pointed at a specific workflow or UI problem in the reporter itself.

Research

Structured interviews with QA leads and player community managers to understand how the existing reports were being used downstream. The goal was to design for the consumer of the report as much as the submitter — what did a triage engineer actually need to see to act on a report quickly?

Iteration

Three rounds of prototype testing with internal QA staff before any player-facing changes. Each round tested a specific hypothesis about field requirements, category structure, or submission flow. Changes were small and measurable — not full redesigns.

Outcome

Report completeness (reports with sufficient reproduction information to begin triage) increased from approximately 40% to 78% over two release cycles following the redesign. Duplicate submission rate dropped by half.
