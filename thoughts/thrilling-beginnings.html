Thrilling Beginnings

Web Development
Published June 20, 2026
Updated June 20, 2026

Overview

I collect small things. Functional things, mostly — objects that do one job and do it quietly. Some are physical. Some are digital. All of them have a way of occupying my hands or my attention in the background while I'm thinking. Here are four of them.

The Things

These are not the best tools for their jobs. Some of them are barely tools at all. What they share is that I keep returning to them, which is a kind of proof I find more convincing than any review.

The Physical

A brass bolt that came with a piece of furniture I built. I carry it in my left pocket. I don't know exactly why — it started because the bolt didn't fit, and I meant to return it to the hardware store. That was three years ago. It's the right weight. It's the right temperature. When I'm thinking through a problem, I turn it over in my fingers without noticing.

A pocket notebook. Narrow, unlined, no brand on the cover. I go through one every two or three months. I don't review them. I'm not sure the act of writing is the point — it's more that some thoughts need a place to go before they'll leave me alone.

The Digital

A small menu bar application called Itsycal that lives in the top right corner of my screen and does exactly one thing: shows me the date and a small monthly calendar when I click it. I installed it to replace the macOS clock. I have never opened its preferences. It requires nothing from me and asks for nothing. It is, as far as I can tell, perfect.

A Pomodoro timer I built myself in a spreadsheet three years ago and have never updated. It counts down from 25 minutes, plays a short tone, and resets. It has no features. I use it every day.

The best tools are the ones you stop noticing — except that they're always there when you need them.

— Something I wrote in one of those notebooks
