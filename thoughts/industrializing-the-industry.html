Overview

Game development has industrialized — studios have grown into large organisations with specialised disciplines, defined pipelines, and formal processes borrowed from manufacturing. This is a placeholder for a longer piece on what that shift costs and what it gains.

What Industrialization Means

Placeholder content. When an industry industrializes, craft knowledge gets codified into repeatable process. Some things get faster and more consistent. Other things — the ones that resisted codification — quietly disappear from the work.

What Gets Lost

Placeholder content. The parts of game development that don't fit into a pipeline tend to be the parts that made particular games feel distinctive. This is not an argument against process — it is an argument for noticing what process does to the things it touches.

Where This Is Going

Placeholder content. The pressures that drove industrialization — scale, cost, risk management — are not going away. The question is whether there's a way to preserve space for the work that doesn't scale, alongside the work that does.
